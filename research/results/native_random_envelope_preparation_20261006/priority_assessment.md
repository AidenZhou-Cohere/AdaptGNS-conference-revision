# Additional evidence before the deadline

Prepared October 6, 2026, before inspecting any partial native autonomous accuracy or any paired110k endpoint outcomes. This is a decision memo only: no experiment, inference, fitting or source change was performed. Original100k, graph bridge, actual-action and optional-exposure evidence remain distinct and unchanged.

The current native autonomous cohort and matched faithful base-only versus random graph-exposure continuation remain the most valuable controls. They address deployment behavior and training support, respectively. The proposed additions below must not displace their complete populations, audit or six-hour writing reserve. More experiment count is not an independent contribution.

## Recommended optional addition: repeated random actions on native observed histories

An eight-draw exact-budget random-action envelope is more informative than a graph-only concentration null. It answers a concrete remaining question: **is the observed risk-versus-random ordering robust to which uniformly sampled optional pairs are used?** The existing bridge uses one random draw per unit, different from the original study, and globally orders directed edges. A new native-prefix study can both remove that edge-order mismatch and quantify conditional random-action variability. Neither existing comparison alone estimates the expected uniform-random action accurately.

Use all six unchanged100k checkpoints and all425 fixed observed histories per model, keeping validation128/test297 separate. Freeze eight draw IDs and exactly25% optional-pair budgets, with native capped/self base edges preserved in their original prefix and a separately ordered symmetric optional suffix. Evaluate native base, previous-observed-base-risk and current-observed-base-risk with this same helper. Previous observed risk requires its preceding native base-scoring history and is not autonomous cached own-graph risk. Saved globally sorted bridge risk predictions cannot serve as these native controls. Preserve native/supplied parity, all selected pairs, ties, empty budgets, predictions, failures, source/model/data hashes and measured operational time. A protocol must state the random streams and sharing across models explicitly; paired common draws across objectives are useful, while the draw-ID streams must be independent conditional on each fixed frame's candidate set.

The scientific null contrast is zero conditional mean excess error,

`risk error - E_uniform_exact_budget[random error] = 0`,

for each declared risk rule, objective and split under the fixed trained models and histories. This is an estimand, not a justified population-level hypothesis test. Estimate the expectation by the arithmetic mean of all eight random draws; do not select the best random draw or use the random envelope to tune a policy. For every draw, retain frame/trajectory/seed differences, all signs and both risk comparisons. Average frames within each trajectory, then trajectories within each seed; report all three seed effects. Draw-to-draw Monte Carlo variation and variation across the three trained seeds answer different questions and must be reported separately. Pairing a draw across models does not create additional trained-model replicates.

Useful secondary summaries are the eight-draw range and tie-aware fraction of draws with lower error than each risk rule, at the frame level and separately at the equal-trajectory seed-mean level. Name the level each time. A frame-rank fraction is not interchangeable with the mean error contrast. Eight draws have coarse tails; neither a rank against eight draws nor a permutation-style `(rank+1)/(8+1)` should be presented as a calibrated p-value, a confidence bound on generalization, or evidence of equivalence. Deterministic risk is not exchangeable with a uniform action under an automatically valid randomization null. Include unchanged/empty optional actions and failed-output nulls rather than silently dropping them.

This study would strengthen or qualify the observed ordering, but cannot identify a causal concentration mechanism. Uniform-random actions remain structurally different from max-endpoint risk actions; differing degree concentration, spatial placement and interactions are jointly changed. It also cannot establish autonomous superiority, independent confirmation, training-distribution adequacy, an optimized latency frontier or a new winning policy. New draws and new native inference reuse already inspected histories.

**Cost and sequencing.** The completed six-model bridge performed40,800 cases in693.156s of model-process wall time, including startup/I/O/checks. Eight random actions plus native base/current/previous-risk and required parity/scoring calls at2,550 histories are a comparable forward-pass workload. A10–30min execution allowance is plausible, not measured or guaranteed; implementation, synthetic tests, independent audit and concise integration likely require another1–3h of attention. This dominates a nominal seconds-only graph null but buys substantially more scientific information.

Prepare only if a separate agent can do so while the native cohort runs without delaying its audit or the paired110k path. Launch sequentially after native completion only if the entire fixed study, summary and audit are ready and a conservative allowance still leaves the full paired training/evaluation plan inside its reviewed time gates. Do not wait for an unfinished optional implementation when paired training is ready. Otherwise reconsider after endpoint evaluation; do not squeeze it into the writing reserve, reduce draws/population after looking at values, or run it concurrently with the main queue.

## Alternatives and their incremental value

| Addition | New information and honest reference | Full cost and recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Graph-only risk permutation / concentration reference | Whether observed score placement yields coverage/degree concentration different from random assignment of the same score multiset on fixed geometry. An unconditional permutation tests a chosen score-to-node random-assignment reference; geometry-stratified permutations change that reference. They do not automatically preserve degree concentration or yield an exchangeable inferential null. Without predictions they cannot test accuracy or show concentration causes the deficit. | Cheap geometry/CPU work, but source/protocol/audit/figure work still costs time. Lower value than repeated random-action inference now. Defer unless a precise structural claim survives that the existing exposure and degree results cannot answer. |
| Saved-output damping curve | The prediction blend has benefit `alpha*A-alpha^2*C`; its risk-minus-random gap is `alpha^2*DeltaC-alpha*DeltaA`. The inspected positive `DeltaC>DeltaA>0` already implies a zero crossing. No new causal or generalization hypothesis is tested by plotting the identity. | Almost free arithmetic, modest editorial value, no priority as an experiment. A training-only fitted scalar plus autonomous evaluation would be genuinely new but introduces fitting, two-pass runtime and validation obligations; defer. |
| Boundary-excursion onset / failure curve from saved per-step records | Existing summaries report mean outside fractions, excursions, fixed MSE horizons and failure prefixes; the inspected rollout analysis does not yet summarize first excursion onset. A fixed graph-scale threshold (e.g. excursion greater than the already fixed base radius0.015) could show when predictions first depart substantially from metadata bounds, with identical truth references. This is a descriptive geometric event, not a test of physical validity. | No inference; seconds–minutes CPU plus perhaps1–2h for robust failure-aware implementation/audit/plotting. Useful second optional choice after native completion if it makes an important deployment limitation clearer. Freeze the threshold before new calculations; never choose a favorable horizon. |
| Per-trajectory influence from completed paired scalar records | Existing records retain all paired trajectory differences, but the inspected reporting does not summarize leave-one-trajectory-out influence. It could establish whether a mean contrast is dominated by one observed trajectory. Its reference is the complete finite set of recorded trajectory means, not new experimental replication. | Very cheap arithmetic and a small table/plot; classify as robustness reporting, not a new experiment. Add only to clarify an ambiguous complete-cohort result, retaining every omitted-trajectory contrast and the original point estimate. |

For the onset option, keep all27 trajectories in every denominator. Report first crossing and numerical failure separately, including a failed-before-crossing category; numerical failure is not benign right-censoring and survivors must not become the cohort. A truth crossing uses the same future frames and threshold. A trajectory that crosses and later fails retains both facts. Do not call the threshold a certified usable horizon, infer conservation, or make a significance claim from correlated particle/frame counts. Native results must first be complete; new curves cannot justify selectively stopping or interpreting the current partial queue.

## Decision

Protect native completion and the paired110k intervention first. If one additional inference study fits with full validation, choose the fixed repeated-random native observed envelope. If only CPU/reporting time remains, a tightly scoped onset/failure figure or all-trajectory influence display can add clarity; a broad new physical-descriptor correlation table would largely repeat existing analysis. Defer graph-only permutation nulls, learned gates, budget searches, additional objectives and new-domain training. Favor a clear, supported explanation and complete adverse-result accounting over a late unreviewed result.
