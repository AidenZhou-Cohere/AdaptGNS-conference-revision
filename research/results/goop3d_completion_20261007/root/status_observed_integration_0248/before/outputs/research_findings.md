All fifteen frozen checkpoints were diagnosed on the same 36 validation and 36 test frames. Validation shows the same qualitative gap: risk–error correlations .241/.414/.330, versus risk–benefit .007/−.029/−.030. Benefits sum both coordinates, so divide by two to compare with coordinate MSE. Benefit contains base error algebraically; correlation alone cannot establish useful allocation. **Decision:** make the next research target the signed, model-specific value of extra edges per cost, with an option to add none. Use uncertainty as a baseline. A learned benefit score is a testable proposal, not yet a validated new algorithm or a novelty claim. ## 5. Mathematical and implementation corrections

The released log-variance coefficient is 0.5 instead of d/2, and its Softplus output is treated as standard deviation before squaring, unlike the paper's variance description. Evaluation also replaces training normalization with a zero-noise scale; WATERDROP acceleration scales differ by factors 10.285 and 8.471. Corrected code now preserves explicit legacy semantics, reconstructs architecture and normalization from version-2 checkpoints, respects validation/test splits, handles baseline loading and graph conventions, and repairs calibration sampling/ties. An explicit CPU SciPy graph backend enables actual execution.

Sixteen targeted full-code tests and a real two-update original-architecture CLI smoke run passed; the pilot has six targeted tests, the risk–benefit diagnostic three, the new benefit head three, and autonomous-rollout verification six. The smoke run is integration evidence, not scientific training. CUDA, distributed execution and the inherited full upstream suite remain unverified. Native MPS execution of the full-size GNS is feasible, with measured graph/transfer-inclusive Adam medians of 68.85/90.00 ms for 678/1,033 particles. Strict gradient parity fails for four of eight examined histories, so the 9.56/12.50-hour arithmetic projections for 500k updates are not completed training or identical-optimization guarantees. The production backend and a 100-update MPS runner stop/resume check now pass, followed by a final-source five-update check and 13 runner contract tests. The clean validation MSE rose from .02113 to .05578 during the 100-update smoke, so it is integration evidence, not demonstrated learning progress. The requested six-model 100k suite is complete; its paired validation endpoints are recorded below. The historical 500k multi-dataset benchmark has not been reproduced. The CPU graph-only experiment found median budgeted-construction cost 1.542× sparse construction and node-percentile cost 1.666× on nine states with synthetic scores, underscoring why edge totals cannot substitute for runtime.

For fixed model mean, minimizing isotropic Gaussian expected NLL gives variance equal to the conditional residual second moment divided by dimension, subject to positivity/floor constraints. This is not automatically physical noise or epistemic uncertainty. Same-state expansion preserves the base graph; rollout cost changes also contain a geometry term. Under an explicitly additive gain surrogate, uniform score error δ gives top-B regret at most 2Bδ; a decomposition δ=a(ε+Dt)+ζ makes score error, lag and surrogate mismatch visible. None of this supplies a simulator stability guarantee, and time-correlated errors matter for rollout growth.

Faithful regression and β-NLL are existing methods. LAMP and other adaptive graph/mesh methods preclude a broad dynamic-graph novelty claim. The revised contribution must be positioned at the particular allocation mechanism and its evidence, with faithful regression credited to Stirn et al. (AISTATS 2023) and β-NLL to Seitzer et al. (ICLR 2022).

## 6. Concrete submission decisions

1. **Do not submit the original superiority claim.** It is contradicted by the historical long horizon and unsupported by the small new pilot.
2. **A diagnostic/reproducibility version is the defensible near-term option** if the authors accept a changed contribution, verify provenance, and present the convenience-sample limits prominently. These local results alone do not guarantee conference acceptance.
3. **For an algorithmic conference paper, the next decisive step is a larger, protocol-locked evaluation of graph value:** use the completed ridge candidate as a minimal baseline, improve the match between its training intervention and actual budgeted actions, and compare against random/speed/risk in autonomous rollouts. A stronger baseline and the value-of-edges hypothesis take priority over inventing another variance loss.
4. **Freeze the next protocol before using new test data.** Retain full provenance, multiple seeds, actual horizons, physical diagnostics and end-to-end timing. Until then, frame the benefit predictor as exploratory and retain negative results.
